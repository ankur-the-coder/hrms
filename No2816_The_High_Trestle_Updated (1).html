Built to look closer.

An updated reconstruction of No. 2816 - The Rocky Mountain Express, based on the supplied High Trestle scene and reference photographs.

  • Motion, not a floating shaft. Coupling rods join all three driving axles. The main connecting rod drives a guided crosshead using slider-crank geometry. Opposite cranks are quartered by 90 degrees.
  • Two different housings. Each side has a lower power cylinder and an upper piston-valve chest, covered by shaped, riveted jackets. Remove the jackets to follow the piston and valve rods.
  • Railway wheelsets throughout. Profiled treads and inboard flanges, spoked counterweighted drivers, dished leading wheels and detailed trailing and tender trucks.
  • The details that ground it. Compact open pilot, Elesco heater, compressor and feed pump, complete piping, brake rigging, sand pipes, drawbars, couplers, safety chains, cab steps and tender ladders.
  • The original valley. Track spacing, sleepers, bridge panels, lattice bents, shader terrain, river, distant bridge, skyline, weather and seven cinematic shots follow the shared High Trestle source.
  • Safe ray tracing. The optional preview starts at one bounce, quarter resolution and eight samples. A triangle budget excludes the entire valley and passenger consist.
